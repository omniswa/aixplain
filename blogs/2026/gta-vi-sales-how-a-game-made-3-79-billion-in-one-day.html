GTA VI Sales: How a Game Made $3.79 Billion in One Day

GTA VI reportedly made $3.79 billion in 24 hours. Here’s what the record really measures, what the numbers leave out, and why 13 years of waiting paid off.

By xclnt-sensai | Published Nov 28, 2026 | 4-minute read

A baby born the week Grand Theft Auto V hit shelves is now thirteen years old, taller than the family dog, and almost certainly not allowed to play its sequel.

That’s how long the gap lasted. GTA V arrived in September 2013 and has since sold nearly 235 million units, so the wait for GTA VI wasn’t a delay so much as a generational event. On November 19 it ended, and the money showed up as if it had been camping outside the store since 2013.

The number everyone is quoting

Rockstar says GTA VI generated $3.79 billion in sales in its first 24 hours, which it calls the biggest entertainment launch in history. For scale, GTA V’s first day was estimated at around $800 million, according to Reuters, so the sequel did nearly five times the business. It also edged past the roughly $2.94 billion that Avengers: Endgame earned across its entire worldwide run.

Read that again. One day of a video game, against the whole theatrical life of one of the highest-grossing films ever made.

Now the fine print, because this column has a code of honor. Fox Business itself notes that lifetime box-office receipts and first-day retail sales are different revenue measures. Retail sales are what customers paid, not what the publisher keeps after retailers and platforms take their cut. And a movie ticket costs a fraction of a new game, so a game needs far fewer customers to reach a giant number.

“Games beat movies” is the headline. “A smaller crowd paid much more per head” is the footnote. Neither makes the number small.

Hollywood had a rough week

The same month, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, a legitimate blockbuster, opened to about $225 million globally and was projected to earn more than $85 million in North America over the five-day Thanksgiving period.

By my math, GTA VI’s first day was roughly seventeen times that global opening. It’s apples to oranges again, but it’s a very large orange. A film studio’s biggest fall event spent the week as the opening act.

Yes, people still bought discs

In 2026, “owning” a game mostly means owning a login. Shoppers apparently didn’t get the memo. NielsenIQ reports that UK games retail revenue hit £142 million in launch week, up 99% week on week. Boxed software revenue jumped 409% to an estimated £43 million, the biggest week for physical games since Christmas 2018.

GTA VI alone made up £32 million of that, about 74% of physical software revenue, and 88% of its first-week sales came on launch day. That isn’t a sales curve. It’s a stampede with a barcode.

Hardware followed: PlayStation 5 sales rose 46% and Xbox Series sales 75% that week. And that’s one country.

Week one: handle the estimates with oven mitts

The first-week numbers come from a third-party analyst, not the publisher. Alinea Analytics estimates roughly 53 million copies and $4.7 billion in revenue, with PS5 at about 75.7% of sales and Xbox Series X|S at about 24.3%. Official confirmation from Take-Two and Rockstar was still pending as of reports on Friday.

The engagement figures are the fun part. Nearly 7 million players have reportedly logged more than 50 hours, and about 250,000 have passed 100. The first weekend ran straight into US Thanksgiving week, which explains how a quarter of a million people hit triple digits, or at least gives them an alibi.

The game is set in the fictional state of Leonida, where Vice City is inspired by Miami, and crime is the local industry. This week, the real-world version of that industry was retail.

The plot twist: they did it with one hand tied

Here’s what the headline leaves out. GTA VI is out only on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, and no PC version has been announced. Rockstar hasn’t given a timeline for a PC release or the online multiplayer rollout, which some analysts expect in 2027.

So the biggest entertainment launch ever came with a big slice of the audience still waiting outside and the online mode not yet open.

The rest of the report card is just as lopsided. Rockstar says the game sold a record 32.5 million units before release, and it has a reported Metacritic score of 99. Take-Two’s stock jumped more than 6% as the game arrived.

What to argue about at dinner

What are we measuring? “Biggest launch” depends entirely on the ruler. Sales at retail, box office and streaming hours don’t convert into one another, and every industry picks the ruler that flatters it.

How much can one title bend a market? A single game accounted for roughly three-quarters of UK physical software revenue in its launch week. That’s less a market than a weather system.

Does day one even matter? One report put it bluntly: beating GTA V’s lifetime total will take sustained popularity well beyond launch week. Launch day is a flex. Week twenty is the exam.

The takeaway

The world gave us the headline: $3.79 billion. The plot twist is that the headline is the least interesting thing about it.

The interesting part is that a thirteen-year-old franchise, on two consoles, with no PC version and no online mode yet, made the rest of entertainment look like it was still on the tutorial level. Somewhere, a PC gamer is staring at a calendar. The calendar isn’t saying anything either.
